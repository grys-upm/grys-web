The primary goal of the SWARMs project was to expand the use of underwater and surface vehicles (AUVs, ROVs, USVs) to facilitate the conception, planning and execution of maritime and offshore operations and missions, reducing operational costs, increasing the safety of tasks and of the people involved, and expanding the offshore sector.

SWARMs aimed to make AUVs, ROVs and USVs more accessible and useful, making autonomous maritime and offshore operations a viable option for new and existing industries:

- Enabling AUVs/ROVs to work in a cooperative mesh, thus opening up new applications and ensuring re-usability by promoting heterogeneous standard vehicles that can combine their capabilities, instead of further costly specialised vehicles.
- Increasing the autonomy of AUVs/USVs and improving the usability of ROVs for the execution of simple and complex tasks, contributing to more sophisticated mission operations.

The general approach was to design and develop an integrated platform for a new generation of autonomous maritime and underwater operations: a set of software/hardware components, adopted and incorporated into the current generation of maritime and underwater vehicles, to improve the autonomy, robustness, cost-effectiveness and reliability of offshore operations, namely through vehicle cooperation.

SWARMs’ achievements were demonstrated in three field tests covering different scenarios and use cases:

- Corrosion prevention in offshore installations
- Monitoring of chemical pollution
- Detection, inspection and tracking of plumes
- Berm building
- Seabed mapping

SWARMs was an industry-led project, in which large technology companies collaborated with SMEs specialised in the subsea, robotics and communication sectors, and with universities and research institutions, so that state-of-the-art innovations in these domains could rapidly reach the market. Two industrial end-users, also part of the consortium, contributed their perspectives and expectations.
